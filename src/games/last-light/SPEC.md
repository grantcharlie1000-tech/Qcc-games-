# Last Light

Original Canvas 2D retro platformer, inspired by the compact pixel-art presentation of the supplied poteto.com reference. All art and code are original. Forty fixed stages unlock strictly in order, across The Old Quarter, Mossworks, The Clockworks, Frostline, and The Afterglow. There is no endless or randomized mode.

Run, variable-height jump, wall jump and one air dash (landing or a blue crystal refills it). A 120 Hz simulation supplies coyote time and buffered jumps. Moving platforms carry the player; cracked floors collapse and regenerate; springs bounce; ice reduces friction; belts push; spikes, moving saws, and pulsing lasers cause checkpoint respawns. Each stage has three collectible sparks; later worlds require a gold key to pass the gate. Collectibles persist across deaths to prevent farming. R retries with a death penalty. P pauses. Touch buttons support simultaneous movement and actions. Decorative motion freezes under reduced-motion preferences; essential hazard motion continues.

The courier is drawn as a detailed 24×34 pixel silhouette around the original 16×24 collision box: shaded hood and face, layered coat, leather satchel, straps, gloves, boots and a carried lantern. Its eight-frame run cycle follows distance traveled. Distinct idle, rising, falling, wall-slide, dash, landing, respawn and beacon-lighting poses communicate state. The scarf and lantern follow through, dust marks footfalls and landings, and brief dash echoes use fixed-size effect pools. Animation advances with the physics ticks and freezes during pause; reduced motion removes decorative movement, particles and echoes. Retry, stage changes, new runs and restores clear visual history.

The original soundtrack, “A Light Left On,” is a 71-second, 32-bar chiptune composition in A minor at 108 BPM, with a recurring pulse-wave melody, warm chord voicings, arpeggios, bass and synthesized percussion. It follows the existing Sound setting, starts only during play after user interaction, keeps its musical position through pauses and stage transitions, rewinds for a new journey, and stops when hidden or leaving the game. Fetch/decode completion cannot resume music while paused or disposed. The source score and a reproducible offline renderer are included alongside the mastered MP3.

Checkpoints, collected items, stage results, total time, deaths and run best persist through QCC's validated local/cloud save lifecycle. Restoring waits for Continue and respawns safely at the checkpoint. A new run asks for confirmation and keeps the shared personal best. Time advances only during active play and includes death recovery.

Shared account-backed leaderboard: points are awarded only on stage clear. Score = cleared stages × 1,000,000 + clamp(600,000 + sparks × 2,000 − whole active seconds × 10 − deaths × 1,000, 1, 999,999). Each stage therefore outranks every result with fewer cleared stages. At equal progress sparks, speed and fewer deaths improve the result. The existing service keeps each account's maximum score and handles signed-in identity, public/personal boards, offline retries and sync. The new database migration preserves every existing score ID. Hosted migration application is left to the normal deployment process; verification uses local databases.
