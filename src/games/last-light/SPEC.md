# Last Light

Original Canvas 2D precision platformer. Forty authored levels span the Old Quarter, Mossworks, Clockworks, Frostline and Afterglow. Reach the gold exit in each level. Conversations, lamps, rescued residents, departure bells, memory routes and the final story choice are optional. None can lock an exit. The opening teaches variable jump height and air dash. Level 2 begins with three broad landing decks reachable without a dash; later levels combine narrow catches, short hops, braking, collapsing floors, moving platforms, slippery takeoffs, conveyors and pulsing gates.

## Movement and challenge

Run, variable-height jump, wall jump and one air dash, refilled by landing or a blue crystal. Physics runs at 120 Hz with coyote time and buffered jumps. Release direction to brake; holding jump and dashing at the same point cannot serve every crossing. Moving lifts carry horizontally and vertically. Springs launch across their full collision width. Ice reduces friction and belts push. Spikes, moving saws, electrical gates, steam, mounted presses, patrol machines, canal water and the pursuing dark cause checkpoint respawns. End or R retries with one setback; Delete or P pauses. Arrows move and jump, Down drops through metal grates, Right Shift dashes and Enter interacts. Grates can be jumped through from below. Stomp patrol machines or dash through them; side contact causes a setback. Holding jump on a stomp gives a higher bounce. Defeats grant no score and reset on a retry. Arrow keys and Enter also navigate game menus.

Three or four stationary flags per level save the furthest reached checkpoint. Safe flags and optional fixtures have broad decks; transit catches are smaller. Transit platforms under 200px have at most one obstacle, with an open incoming landing zone. Timed combinations use wider decks and stable observation ground, including at 320px width. Amber emitters warn before collision returns. Hazard duty and crumble delay are authored per encounter. Crumble faces separate along deterministic irregular fractures, shed dust, fall as textured stone chips and rebuild before collision returns. Fractures split the material itself; no crack overlay or equal vertical slices are drawn. Supported spikes and machinery fragment with the floor and stop colliding until it reforms. Reduced motion preserves distinct warning, collapse and rebuild states while suppressing decorative motion.

Five escape levels increase base pursuit from 88 to 134 world pixels per second. The dark begins after leaving the safe opening, resets behind the checkpoint and accelerates continuously when far behind, up to 440 pixels per second. It never teleports. The HUD shows the remaining lead and changes colour when close.

## Story on the side

The courier follows their missing sister Mira through a city waiting for dawn. Optional conversations and 120 distinct memory fragments explain the lantern, Mira and the dawn engine. E interacts only when nearby; touch uses the contextual prompt instead of an always-present HELP control. Wheels shut down linked machinery with visible green indicators; players may instead cross the active timed gate. Manual conversations pause until dismissed or movement resumes. Memory pickups save quietly to the journal without opening dialogue.

The primary HUD shares a single 44px glass row with Games and Menu. The canvas fills the entire game view behind it on desktop and phone, including behind touch controls. Level progress, points and memories occupy a compact glass island; timer, setbacks and full level names live in pause/results screens. Detailed checkpoint and district information sits in the pause menu. There is no route map or map shortcut. Flags and visible landings guide progression. J or Page Down opens the optional journal, containing only collected memories and actually heard conversations; the journal pauses play.

Completion banks points and freezes active time immediately, then plays a skippable 1.25–2.2s physical departure. Ferries and airships board, settle and accelerate fully beyond the viewport while the camera stays at the dock. Lifts belong to a masonry service tower: the courier boards, rides to a distinct upper landing, walks out and enters its doorway. Signal, relay, tree and dawn exits activate their fixture, then lead the courier through a real onward stairwell or garden gateway. Shelter and rescue doors close after entry; escape routes lead through a threshold. Enter, Space, E or a touch action skips to results and Next level. No mandatory chapter aftermath or cinematic bars interrupt the playfield. Completing all forty levels shows campaign results first; reading and choosing the story ending is optional and can be left for later. The narrative has two hopeful conclusions.

Five signature chapters (4, 11, 19, 27 and 35) use overlapping streets, spring canopies, press/lift shafts, a vertical ice hollow and twin towers. Their main and optional paths retain the campaign’s required changes of jump/dash control. See [design sources and encounter intentions](LEVEL_DESIGN.md).

## World and presentation

Foreground materials and actors share the courier's native pixel scale: shaded faces, layered clothing, occupation props, wood joinery, ceramic caps, damp masonry, riveted iron, bronze and fractured ice. World-space building faces, windows, columns, brackets and pipe risers follow the actual ledges; distant scenery supplies depth. Brick materials cache only small repeating tiles. Walkable deck edges remain brighter than background construction. Foreground facades have bevelled brick faces, recessed framed windows, mullions, shutters, sills and connected drainpipes. Terrain polygons and diagonal material lines use hard pixel rasterisation to match the courier and fixtures. Two parallax planes add clock towers, inhabited roofs, viaducts and foundry cranes. Mossworks, Frostline and Afterglow use separate native-pixel trees, glasshouses, ridges, monasteries, observatories, spires and viaducts. Each district has several independent parallax speeds, with mist between the distant scenery and closer landmarks. Original generated environment/model sheets now supply palette-limited, nearest-neighbour district vistas and 32×32 material samples. Cached pixel textures join the terrain and recessed façades. Hand-authored actors use the model sheet's tailoring, leatherwork, ochre lining and occupational clothing. Original procedural scenery remains the loading/failure fallback and the cavern composition. Support walls and glacial shoulders end at the authored world cutoff; fog starts below the lowest landing in world coordinates. Canal water keeps its reflections. See `docs/art/last-light/ART_DIRECTION.md` for sources, generation references and the reproducible conversion pipeline.

Vents originate in bolted grates. Vapor thins into a feathered upper plume; after the emitter closes, residual wisps move upward and fade. The dangerous jet ends when the emitter stops, while the small trailing plume is cosmetic. Electrical gates have mounted upper and lower contacts with a rear return arm. Compact pressure regulators replace oversized floating wheels. Older keepers have a shaped grey beard, soft work cap, rust scarf and weathered coat; elders retain a distinct wool-and-shawl silhouette. Residents and decorations retain stable opacity at close range. Stand beside a cat and press Enter/E or tap its contextual prompt to pet it. A crouch/reach, three strokes, head/ear/tail reaction and small pixel hearts last 1.25s; movement, jumping, dashing or retry cancels immediately. Petting grants no score, quest progress or saved state. All canal ferries are moored beyond a descending solid dock, with their lower hull masked by water. Composition reserves the foreground silhouettes and travel sweeps of terrain, pickups, hazards, fixtures and actors. Trees and habitat dressing remain sparse to preserve sightlines. The library poster uses the shared isometric voxel generator.

The canvas fills the view behind the single floating glass row. Desktop rendering uses integer pixel scaling near a 360-world-pixel height; short landscape and phone views preserve native pixel aspect. Touch permits simultaneous movement and jump/dash with four persistent controls; optional interaction appears only in context. Decorative movement freezes under reduced motion; mechanical collision timing remains consistent.

## Points and persistence

Each cleared level banks 1,000 + 100 per spark collected there + max(0, 250 − whole active seconds × 2 − setbacks × 25). Later levels cannot remove earlier bonuses. Maximum score: 62,000. Results explain the calculation. Old million-scale leaderboard/local/cloud bests convert to 1,000 per earned level because separate bonuses cannot be recovered from the former sort key; known saved spark bonuses are preserved. Other games are unchanged. Shared server-side score storage has a separately documented backend migration.

Version 2 saves validate level progress, discoveries, encounter IDs, checkpoint index and optional ending. Earlier preview saves preserve level progress and discoveries, including migration away from the obsolete escape lamp requirement. Saves resume only after Continue. A new run asks for confirmation and preserves the personal best. Music, sound, focus, visibility, pause and disposal follow the shared arcade lifecycle.

## Verification approach

Continuous legal-input recordings are replayed independently through the real 120 Hz model for all forty levels, with interaction disabled. Local reachability checks cover main and optional paths; composition checks cover headroom and artwork clearance. A separate bounded policy audit samples a generous repeated full jump and midair dash across different takeoffs and platform phases, including optional shortcuts and ignoring hazards. It checks that precision sections require control changes; it is not an exhaustive proof over all possible techniques. Recorded routes establish playability, not average human completion time. Browser checks exercise actual keyboard events, touch, rendering, overlays, audio and saves.

## Death feedback and frame stability

Each fatal source records its actual impact before checkpoint relocation. A 60 ms contact hold leads into authored hurt/brace poses: spikes, saws, steam and enemies recoil away from contact; electricity removes native-pixel rows; water splashes and submerges the courier; darkness consumes the silhouette. The body stays whole and on the native pixel grid, without rotations, stretching or a screen-wide flash. Falls begin within the last 40 world pixels, below every playable landing, so the reaction remains visible before sinking beyond the cutoff. The nine reactions and checkpoint-local rekindling fit a 0.48-second recovery. Reduced motion uses a stationary fading pose. Pause freezes animation age; manual retry and stage changes clear stale feedback. Supported hazards stop colliding when their floor crumbles.

The render loop changes HUD text and attributes only when their values change. Stable viewport dimensions do not reallocate the canvas or reset its camera, and retries retain the terrain texture cache. Camera look-ahead eases through turns rather than flipping instantly. These reduce unnecessary layout work and respawn hitches without changing the 120 Hz movement simulation.
