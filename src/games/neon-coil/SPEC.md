## Rules and tuning

Every tunable is an exported, commented constant in `tuning.ts`; the model, renderer, input and tests all read it.

- **Arena (+20% area).** Circular radius `LEGACY_ARENA_RADIUS × √1.2` ≈ 1,643 world pixels (`ARENA_RADIUS`), so playable area is 1.2× the former 1,500-px arena with the same circular proportions. Ordinary food is replenished toward 1,800 (`FOOD_TARGET`, 1,500 × 1.2) and the pellet cap is 3,120 (`MAX_FOOD`), so density is unchanged. Opening bot rings (≈789 / 1,024 / 1,260 px) and the respawn rim margin (≈493 px) scale linearly; the respawn distance from the player stays 600 px because it is camera-relative. Camera zoom is unchanged. Crossing the rim defeats the head.
- **Fixed 60 Hz physics.** Normal speed 165 world pixels/second; boost 1.7× for 6 mass/second, never below 20 mass, emitting one-mass pellets along the tail. Every head position contributes to the body's arc-length history, and the body follows that curved trail exactly. Above 2,048 points, older history is resampled at uniform arc-length intervals while the most recent 64 points stay exact. A long frame catches up at most 250 ms (15 ticks).
- **Steering law (shared by every input and by bots).** The head turns toward a target angle at `min(MAX_TURN_RATE, |error| × TURN_RESPONSE)` = `min(4.4 rad/s, |error| × 12/s)`, never overshooting. Large turns run at the capped rate (a U-turn takes about 0.8 s, up from 3.2 rad/s before); errors below about 21° ease in proportionally (error halves every ~58 ms), so small corrections are precise and finger or mouse jitter does not wobble the head. Turns take the short way across ±π.
- **Growth.** The player begins at 30 mass. Body target length is `130 + 3 × mass` through 600 mass, then `1930 + 1800 × log1p((mass − 600) / 600)`. Mass continues beyond 600, bounded only by JavaScript safe-integer precision.
- **Hitboxes equal visible bodies.** `radius()` is the body radius (8 px, growing 0.014 per mass point above 30, capped at +4). The head disc is `radius × 1.1` (`headRadius`). The body tapers to 45% over the last 3 diameters of the tail (`bodyRadiusAt`). The renderer sizes every body stamp and head sprite from these same functions; the sprite padding is compensated so the visible disc radius equals the collision radius. Head-to-body contact uses the exact envelope of the tapered discs, with no hidden slack; the rim uses the head radius. Unit tests check that every drawn disc matches the hitbox at several masses, and that contact flips from a near miss to a hit within ±0.05 px of where the drawn discs touch.
- **Collisions.** A head touching another snake's body is defeated. Simultaneous head-on contact defeats both, regardless of size. Self-crossing is safe. The same rules apply to bots and the player.
- **Spawn immunity.** Every spawn (player start, restart via a fresh arena, every bot at arena creation and on respawn) gets `SPAWN_IMMUNITY_SECONDS` = 3 s (180 ticks) of protection. Decisions: an immune head cannot be defeated by other bodies or by head-on contact, **and** an immune snake's body and head are non-lethal to others (fair and readable: a pale snake never kills or dies by contact). **The rim still defeats immune snakes.** Immune snakes eat, boost and hoover normally. The countdown runs only while the arena runs. The protected snake is drawn pale (a 68% pale overlay) and translucent (35% less opacity, composited as one layer so overlaps do not stack), then eases back to normal colour over the final 0.75 s with a smoothstep. Immunity is stored per snake as remaining ticks; saves without the field restore as 0 (unprotected), and values above the current timer are clamped.
- **Dot hoover.** A pellet whose centre is within `HOOVER_RADIUS` (44 px, plus 4 px per px of body-radius growth, so at most 60 px) of a living head is claimed by the nearest such head (lower ID breaks exact ties). It then flows toward that head, starting at 90 px/s and accelerating at 1,500 px/s² up to 900 px/s, which is fast enough to catch a boosting snake. It counts as collected, adding its mass exactly once, when it is within 6 px of the head centre. Pellets outside the radius stay put. When the pellet cap forces an eviction, pellets in flight are never chosen. If the owner dies, the pellet stops where it is and becomes free again. Bots follow the same rules. Pull state (`owner`, `speed`) is part of the deterministic world and of saves; pellets saved before the hoover load unchanged. Hoovered pellets shrink slightly as they arrive.
- **Defeat.** A defeat redistributes the snake's current mass along its actual body at roughly 17-pixel intervals. Other living snakes can collect these pellets. The final player death freezes the arena and shows its reason, mass, time, best and outmaneuver count. Restart creates a fresh seeded world, so the player is protected again.
- **Bots** use the same motion, steering law, growth, hoover, immunity and collision model. Every 12 ticks they choose a reachable curved trajectory using nearby food value, other bodies and the arena boundary. Defeated bots wait 3 simulated seconds, then respawn away from the player and occupied paths, protected for 3 s.
- Difficulty is a fixed **Classic** mode. Ranking sorts actual current mass and includes YOU separately outside the displayed top 10 (top 5 on narrow or short screens). A small map shows live positions on larger viewports.

## Input and lifecycle

Every input proposes only a **target angle**; the shared steering law above turns the head, so all inputs feel alike.

- **Touch (mobile first).** Touch devices (coarse pointer, or the first real touch on a hybrid device) show a visible thumb stick: a 132-CSS-px ring (`JOYSTICK_RADIUS` 66) with a 52-px knob, resting bottom-left inside the safe-area insets. A thumb landing anywhere in the lower-left zone (left 60% of the width, lower 60% of the height) re-centres the ring under it, never further into the corner than the resting spot. Deflection is measured from where the thumb landed, so touching down never steers by itself, even in the corner. The knob follows the thumb and clamps at 50 px of travel. Direction is analogue (full 360°, from the knob angle) with a dead zone of 14% of travel. Lifting the thumb keeps the last direction the stick gave, so a quick flick completes its turn (or the current heading, if the stick never left the dead zone). Touches outside the zone do not steer. The first thumb in the zone owns steering until it lifts. The Boost button keeps its own captured pointer, so either finger may go first, and steering can be re-acquired while Boost stays held. On touch layouts Boost moves to the right thumb (above the host toolbar row, or beside it on short landscape screens), the hint sits beside or above the stick and fades while steering, the minimap is lifted above the stick, and the stick clears the host Top scores toggle. Desktop layout and Boost visibility are unchanged.
- **Mouse / trackpad.** Pointer movement steers immediately, without a click or drag: the target is the angle from the head (screen centre) to the pointer, with a 20-CSS-px centre dead zone.
- **Keyboard: hold-to-turn.** While arrows or WASD are held, the target is their combined 8-way direction (diagonals included). Releasing every direction key locks the target to the heading at that moment. A short tap is therefore a small, precise nudge, holding completes the turn, and any heading in 360° is reachable. This replaced sticky absolute directions because it gives keyboards fine control and every reachable heading.
- Holding Space boosts when gameplay has keyboard focus; the real Boost button also accepts held Enter/Space. P delegates pause/resume to the host toolbar. Escape is owned by the host and opens Study Mode (unchanged).

All toolbar, start, replay and boost controls are real focusable elements. Controls prevent gameplay scrolling; editable controls and open dialogs keep normal keyboard behaviour. Hidden tabs pause through the host. Pausing clears held inputs and returns the stick to rest; resuming keeps the head's last heading. One requestAnimationFrame chain and one ResizeObserver are disposed on exit. The stick's knob return animation is skipped under reduced motion.

Full world snapshots (food including hoover pull state, bot histories, immunity, RNG, ticks and mass) are persisted through the existing host storage contract. Valid saved running arenas wait behind **Continue arena**; continuing is not a new spawn, so saved immunity simply resumes. Corrupt or incompatible snapshots are rejected; older snapshots without immunity or pull fields load. Best mass stays in the existing per-game high-score store.

## Performance and verification boundaries

Canvas 2D and a spatial hash handle bodies. Rendering culls offscreen food and snakes; glow sprites and the original background tile are created once. Pixel density caps at 2×. Game code and styles load lazily; the existing offline manifest automatically includes the new chunk and cover.

See `TEST_RESULTS.md` and `docs/evidence/neon-coil/` for actual measurements. Synthetic mouse events cover the desktop pointer path; no physical trackpad testing is claimed. Chromium CDP covers native multi-touch events. Browser/encoder download failures and the pending independent review are recorded explicitly.
