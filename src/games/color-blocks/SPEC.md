# Color Blocks

An endless, fixed-orientation 8×8 block-placement puzzle. This is a Block Blast-style tray game, distinct from Block Fill's finite silhouette puzzles and unrelated to match-three.

## Rules and research

Read on 2026-10-19: [RisticDjordje/BlockBlast-Game-AI-Agent README](https://github.com/RisticDjordje/BlockBlast-Game-AI-Agent/blob/main/README.md), retrieved from its raw GitHub URL. Its introduction describes an 8×8 board, three randomly supplied shapes, completed rows/columns and successive-clear combos. Its human controls use selection, hover preview and placement. This is a secondary implementation reference, not an official scoring specification; no code or art was copied. The Apple App Store listing request at https://apps.apple.com/us/app/block-blast/id1617391485 was blocked by network policy. Fixed orientations, exact scoring and fair-deal policy below are explicit product choices under the assigned brief.

- There are 64 logical integer cells and nineteen fixed shapes ranging from a dot to a 3×3 square. Shapes cannot rotate or mirror during play. Colour is decorative.
- A tray holds three pieces. Used slots stay empty until all three are used. The next tray contains exactly three pieces.
- Placement must put every shape cell in bounds on empty cells. Invalid placements do nothing. Pieces never fall or move after placement.
- Identify all full rows and columns from the same post-placement board, then remove the union of cells. Intersections clear once while both lines count.
- Scoring follows the block puzzle systems reference pack (6 November 2026), scoring version 1. Each legal placement adds `N + 20L² + 10L(k − 1) + 40C + 200A`: N placed cells; L full rows plus columns found on the same post-placement board; k the combo chain, capped at 10 for points; C distinct colours present immediately after placement whose tile count becomes zero after the clear; A one when that clear empties the board. With no clear, L, C and A are zero. A crossing tile clears once while its row and column count as two lines. The pack's three worked examples (24, 166, 389) are unit tests.
- Combo is counted in moves, not seconds. A clearing placement increments the chain (the first clear has k = 1) and restores two setup moves, shown as two pips. One or two consecutive non-clearing placements spend a pip and keep the chain; the third resets it. Pausing, saving and restoring leave it unchanged.
- Pieces carry a colour chosen by the seeded game RNG when the tray is dealt, because colour affects points. Cosmetic effects use `Math.random`, never the game RNG.
- Trays are built constructively (generator version 1). Each attempt copies the board and, three times, samples a shape (weighted, with a mild penalty for shapes in the last three trays) from the shapes that have a legal placement, gives it a seeded colour, picks one of its legal positions uniformly and applies the simultaneous clear. Twenty-four attempts are made; if none yields three pieces the search widens by ninety-six more. Complete attempts are scored by board signals (`analyse`: mobility, shapes that fit, cells in one- or two-cell fragments, large-shape room, near-complete lines) combined into an `ease` value. Candidates whose final board falls in the 0.35–0.92 band are preferred, otherwise any complete candidate is kept; a tray whose witness clears a line is mildly favoured (×1.5) without being guaranteed. The tray order is shuffled and the witness remapped.
- The witness (`[slot, x, y]` per piece, in order) is saved with the tray and proves at deal time that all three pieces can be placed, including clears between them. The player may choose another order and strand themselves. If the widened search still finds no complete path, the longest proven prefix is padded with dots; this is not a claim that no path exists. A fully occupied board yields no hand. Existing or partially used saved trays are preserved exactly.
- Saves (version 2) store the seed, generator and scoring versions, mode (`standard`), RNG state and move history. `replay(seed, moves)` rebuilds the identical board, tray and score. Version-one saves are migrated in place (fixed colours, combo with two setup moves if a chain was active) with seed 0, meaning that run cannot be replayed from its start. Only standard mode exists; the pack's relaxed mode is not implemented.
- Loss is derived solely by checking all 64 anchors of every remaining tray piece. A blocked first piece never causes loss while a later piece fits. No timer, rotation, undo, paid continuation or external calls.

## Input and presentation

Rewards follow a hierarchy. A legal placement gets a short snap (90 ms) and a quiet `+N`. While dragging, the rows and columns the drop would clear light up in the piece's colour, using the same rule as release; three or more lines, or a fifth consecutive combo, switches the preview, sweep and label to a rainbow tier (visual only). On release the board, score and rewards commit once. Removed cells are drawn in a separate effects layer, sweeps run 150 ms for one line and 210 ms for several (crossing lines sweep in alternate directions), a bounded tail of up to 24 sparks fades over 300–450 ms, and one label appears for 500–650 ms. Label priority: new best or all clear, then colour removal, then combo, then praise by line count (Good, Great, Excellent, Amazing); lower-priority items and the points join one secondary line. A row above the board shows the combo chip with its setup pips, a compact breakdown of the last move (for example `+166 = 6 blocks + 80 lines + 40 combo + 40 colour`) and, after the run passes the stored best for the first time, a stable New best badge that pulses once. The dock's Score mirrors a total that counts toward the committed score over 300 ms with requestAnimationFrame; `setScore` is called once per change with the committed value. The next drag works while effects fade; pause (including Study Mode), restart, restore and disposal cancel them, and no effect changes state. Reduced motion uses opacity pulses only: no particles, scaling or count-up. When no piece fits, a result card shows score, best, lines, best combo, biggest clear and pieces, with Play again.


The parent provides a viewport-filling stage and compact HUD. The centered module reserves its top 76px, uses the parent `--scene-width`, a flat #30223b background and square dark 8×8 inset. No banner, rounded card frames or movement/placement control panel is present. The tray beneath the board has three clear hit regions and half-board-cell artwork; pickup immediately renders the entire fixed shape at the actual board cell scale. Original SVG cubes have straight faces, light top/left bevels and dark bottom/right bevels. Colourblind marks remain available.

A drag anchors an occupied constituent cell. Mouse/pen keep that cell at the pointer; touch lifts it by `max(26px, 1.25 × current cell width)` above the finger. `input.ts` maps both movement and release through the same actual board bounding rectangle and touch offset. The ghost contains exactly the integer cells `place` will accept; invalid overlap/bounds show no legal ghost. Valid release snaps immediately; invalid release returns the artwork to its tray over 180ms without altering state. A cancelled pointer, lost capture, Escape, pause, blur or resize clears the gesture/selection and never places it. Only one primary pointer owns a gesture.

Alternative: select a tray button then tap the board's top-left destination cell. Keyboard: 1/2/3 select, arrows aim, Enter/Space place; native tray-button activation also selects. Repeated held placement keys cannot commit another piece. A labelled ARIA grid reports occupancy/preview; every tray piece has an accessible shape, block-count and colour/pattern label. Instructions/status remain available to assistive technology without a large visible panel.

WAAPI effects animate only transform/opacity, respect system/product reduced-motion preferences and are cancelled on lifecycle interruption. Keyboard placement is immediate and plays no effects. Sounds remain through the shared context. No original reference image is bundled as artwork.

## State and lifecycle

`model.ts` is renderer/input independent. A version-two snapshot stores board, tray, tray colours, witness, recent shapes, seed, PRNG state, versions, mode, move history, moves, score, lines, combo, setup moves and run records (best combo, biggest clear). `restoreGame(unknown)` validates shapes, colours, dimensions, ranges, moves and unresolved full lines, then returns defensive copies. Invalid or incompatible saves are ignored. Preview/drag is ephemeral and cancelled on pause, restore or restart.

The frozen GameModule exposes pause/resume/restart/serialize/restore/dispose. Pause rejects all actions; restart resets even while paused, with the parent deciding when to resume. Disposal aborts every event listener and releases pointer capture. A ResizeObserver cancels stale geometry when the board size changes and is disconnected on disposal. WAAPI effects are cancelled and removed on interruption. The only requestAnimationFrame loop is the score count-up, cancelled on pause, restart, restore and disposal. There are no intervals, timeouts, global storage or runtime requests. Rendering publishes scores and status only when they change, so pointer movement does not repeatedly write parent score storage.
